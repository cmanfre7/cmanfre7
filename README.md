## Hey, I'm Colt.

I study medicine and build software. At **PulseMedAI**, I'm the technical co-founder and a hands-on developer, working across the application, the interface, and the infrastructure behind it.

My medical training shapes the questions I bring to engineering: what information matters, where uncertainty remains, and what a clinician needs to make the next decision.

## How I approach the work

**Understand the whole system.** I like following a problem from the user's workflow through the application and into the infrastructure. Decisions at one layer tend to show up somewhere else.

**Look closely at the failure cases.** An overall accuracy number leaves questions unanswered. In clinical AI, I'm especially interested in ambiguous inputs, confident mistakes, and how a system should handle uncertainty.

**Make it useful in practice.** I care about the details that determine whether a tool becomes part of someone's day: clear language, sensible defaults, responsive interfaces, and fewer unnecessary steps.

## Medicine & research

My clinical interests are in orthopedic surgery, and my research spans hip preservation, arthroplasty, surgical anatomy, and medical AI. I've contributed to projects examining recovery after surgery, procedural complications, and how multimodal models interpret medical images.

**Hip preservation & recovery.** I've worked on how sex and post-resection alpha angles relate to patient-reported outcomes after arthroscopic femoroplasty and labral repair, published as an [ISHA 2025 conference abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/jhps/hnaf069.360). My work also includes unilateral tensor fasciae latae hypertrophy after hip preservation surgery complicated by arthrofibrosis.

**Outpatient arthroplasty.** I've contributed to research on joint replacement performed in ambulatory surgery centers for selected Medicare patients, focusing on 90-day safety and patient-reported outcomes.

**Surgical anatomy & safety.** I coauthored a [review of iliosacral screw placement](https://aoao.org/2024/12/06/strategies-and-safety-in-iliosacral-screw-placement/), examining sacral anatomy, preoperative planning, and techniques intended to reduce the risk of L5 nerve injury during fixation.

**Medical AI & imaging.** In a [comparative pilot study](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.108715), we evaluated how teaching examples and a structured interpretation framework affected a multimodal language model's performance on chest radiographs. The study examined both the gains from contextual guidance and the diagnostic limitations that persisted.
